## The answer in 20 seconds

Forced bride abduction exists in Kazakhstan and causes real harm. It has been criminalised.<sup><a href="#claim-C3">C3</a></sup>

But presenting it as a "normal tradition" that Kazakhstanis accept or celebrate misrepresents both the country and the problem. The practice covers a wide spectrum — from pre-agreed theatrical elopement to violent abduction — and Kazakhstani law, urban public opinion, and most institutional actors treat non-consensual abduction as a crime.<sup><a href="#claim-C5">C5</a></sup>

## What is ala qashu?

The Kazakh phrase *ала қашу* (ala qashu — literally "take and run") describes a range of practices involving a man taking a woman to his family's home with the expectation of marriage. The practices differ significantly depending on consent and context:<sup><a href="#claim-C1">C1</a></sup>

- **Consensual elopement:** A couple who have already agreed to marry but whose families object — or who want to avoid the cost of a traditional wedding — stage a theatrical "kidnapping" with the woman's full prior knowledge and cooperation. This form is sometimes depicted in Kazakhstani films and cultural narratives.

- **Social pressure situations:** A woman is taken without full prior agreement but under strong family or social pressure to consent after the fact. Refusing is socially costly.

- **Non-consensual abduction:** A woman is taken without her knowledge or agreement. This is the form addressed by criminal law.

The spectrum matters. Treating all instances as the same act either denies a real harm (if only the theatrical form is acknowledged) or misrepresents the culture (if only the coercive form is presented as universal).

## What does Kazakhstani law say?

Kazakhstan's Criminal Code has been progressively updated to address gender-based violence. In September 2023, legislation added **forced marriage by coercion** (*принуждение к браку*) as a separate criminal offence, distinct from kidnapping or general assault charges under which such cases had previously been prosecuted.<sup><a href="#claim-C3">C3</a></sup>

The change followed advocacy by Kazakhstani civil society organisations and reflected both domestic pressure and international scrutiny of Central Asian gender-based violence statistics.

In February 2026, a court in the Mangistau region issued one of the first documented convictions under the new statute — a man was convicted for kidnapping a woman with the purpose of forcing her into marriage.<sup><a href="#claim-C4">C4</a></sup>

## Why is this claim misleading?

Several common versions of the claim misrepresent the situation in different ways:<sup><a href="#claim-C5">C5</a></sup>

**"Bride kidnapping is a normal Kazakh tradition"** implies the practice is universally accepted or even celebrated. It is not. Urban Kazakhstani public opinion, particularly among younger generations, strongly opposes non-consensual abduction. The legal reform reflects genuine domestic pressure for change.

**"Kazakhstanis just accept it as part of their culture"** collapses the range of attitudes within a diverse society. Documented opposition to forced marriage within Kazakhstan is substantial.

**"It's the same in Kazakhstan as in Kyrgyzstan"** conflates two separate countries. Non-consensual bride kidnapping has been more extensively documented and more widely discussed in international media in the context of Kyrgyzstan; applying those statistics or descriptions to Kazakhstan without distinction is inaccurate.<sup><a href="#claim-C6">C6</a></sup>

## What can be said accurately

- Forced marriage by abduction has occurred in Kazakhstan and caused documented harm.<sup><a href="#claim-C2">C2</a></sup>
- Kazakhstan criminalised forced marriage as a separate offence in 2023 and has prosecuted under the law.<sup><a href="#claim-C3">C3</a></sup><sup><a href="#claim-C4">C4</a></sup>
- Consensual theatrical elopement and non-consensual abduction are different acts that share a name in popular description; they should not be conflated.
- The practice is not presented as acceptable by Kazakhstani law or mainstream urban society.
- Conflating Kazakhstan with Kyrgyzstan in this context is a frequent and significant source of misrepresentation.<sup><a href="#claim-C6">C6</a></sup>
