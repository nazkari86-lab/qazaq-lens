## The answer in 20 seconds

Charyn Canyon is a genuine and impressive geological formation in south-east Kazakhstan. The claim that it is the **world's second-largest canyon** is widely repeated on tourism websites — but no scientific or geographical body has published that ranking, and no methodology for the comparison is ever specified.<sup><a href="#claim-C2">C2</a></sup>

## What is Charyn Canyon?

Charyn Canyon is a river canyon carved by the Charyn River in the Almaty region of south-east Kazakhstan. The most photographed section is the Valley of Castles — a roughly 2 km stretch where erosion has shaped the reddish-orange sandstone into towers and walls resembling a ruined city.<sup><a href="#claim-C1">C1</a></sup>

The canyon system as a whole is longer and more varied: it spans approximately 150 km along the river and includes five distinct sections with different rock formations. Charyn National Park was formally established in 2004 to protect the area.

Geologically, the formation dates to the Paleogene and Neogene periods (roughly 65–2.5 million years ago), with the rock exposed by river erosion over millions of years. The flora and fauna of the canyon — including the Sogdian ash relic forest — are considered ecologically significant.

## Where does the second-largest claim come from?

The claim appears consistently on tourism promotion pages, travel review platforms, and tour operator sites. It is almost always stated without a source. When a comparison is given at all, it is typically "only the Grand Canyon is bigger" — but no measurement to support that comparison is provided.

Qazaq Lens searched for a primary scientific source — a geological survey, geographical database, or academic paper — that ranks Charyn as second. None was found.<sup><a href="#claim-C2">C2</a></sup>

The claim appears to have spread through repetition between tourism sources rather than from an original measurement.

## Why ranking canyons is genuinely difficult

There is no single agreed definition of what makes a canyon the "largest" or "deepest."<sup><a href="#claim-C3">C3</a></sup> The metric chosen changes the answer:

- **By length:** The Grand Canyon (446 km) is longer than Charyn, but the Fish River Canyon in Namibia, the Capertee Valley in Australia, and the Yarlung Tsangpo in Tibet are all debated competitors.
- **By maximum depth:** The Yarlung Tsangpo Grand Canyon in Tibet is the deepest in the world by most measures, at over 6,000 m. The Grand Canyon's maximum depth is about 1,800 m.
- **By volume:** The Grand Canyon holds approximately 4.17 cubic miles of rock removed. But volume estimates for many major canyons are not standardised.
- **By width:** The Capertee Valley in New South Wales, Australia, is wider than the Grand Canyon.

The United States Geological Survey has noted that "largest" depends on the metric used, and that the Grand Canyon's fame outpaces its objective dominance on every single measurement.<sup><a href="#claim-C4">C4</a></sup>

Charyn Canyon, at roughly 150 km in length and 300 m in depth, is not close to the top of any global metric. It is a significant formation within Central Asia, but not on a global ranking by standard geographical measures.

## What can be said accurately

- Charyn Canyon is one of the most striking natural landscapes in Central Asia.
- The Valley of Castles section is geologically and scenically exceptional.
- Charyn has been compared to the Grand Canyon in colour and formation type — but the comparison is aesthetic, not a measurement of size.
- The canyon's relic ash forest (*Fraxinus sogdiana*) is a scientifically notable feature independent of any ranking.
